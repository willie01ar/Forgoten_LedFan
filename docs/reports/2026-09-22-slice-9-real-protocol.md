## Summary so far

`PearlFanEncoder` implements the PEARL PX5939 / PowerTRC protocol from the facts in
pearlfan-rs (MIT/Apache-2.0), reimplemented in Swift with no code from the GPLv3 original.
Its packets equal the reference library's, byte for byte, for five inputs: an empty slot,
"A", a full 26-character message, `HELLO WILLIE`, and two images in slots 0 and 1. The
reference bytes were generated by building pearlfan-rs and drawing our rasterizer's pixel
grids through its own `Frame::draw_point`; the harness, grids and outputs are in
`Tools/PearlFanGolden/`. The transport now opens the device seized as hidapi does, waits
one second per report for an interrupt-IN acknowledgement as the reference does, and logs
each report with what came back. The generation-2 writer stays selectable behind the same
`FanReportEncoding` seam.

## The owner's checklist for the first send

Fan A only. Fan B stays in its box and never receives a byte.

1. **Prepare.** Fan A **switched off**. Plug the **data cable** into the head. Leave the power
   cable out. Tell me "data cable in".
2. **I send.** I confirm the fan enumerates, then drive the app itself: USB fan, slot 1,
   `HELLO WILLIE`, Connect, Send. The app waits up to a second per report for an answer, so
   a silent head takes about 40 seconds; an answering head is much faster. I will say
   **"swap now"** and tell you what came back on the wire.
3. **Swap.** Unplug the data cable. Plug in the power cable. Switch the fan on.
4. **Look**, for a full minute, from the front at eye level, then once from a low angle:
   - **slot 1**: does `HELLO WILLIE` appear? Upright and readable, mirrored, upside down,
     or garbled? All in capitals is expected; our font has no lowercase yet (D18).
   - **slots 2 to 8**: does the rest of the factory demo still cycle, is it gone, or is it
     replaced by something else? This is a protocol fact nobody has.
   - anything unexpected: a flicker, a partial word, a blank pass in the cycle.
5. **Report exactly what you saw**, in those terms. Then leave the fan on power; do not put
   the data cable back unless I ask.
